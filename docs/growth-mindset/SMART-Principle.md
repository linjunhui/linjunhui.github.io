# SMART Principle

## Mindset 024｜SMART Principle — Explained

**In one sentence**: A framework for turning a *vague wish* ("I want to get better") into a *testable goal* ("I will ... by ...") — so you can actually judge whether you're on track.

SMART is an acronym where **each letter is a test** your goal must pass:

| Letter | Stands for | Question it asks | ❌ Weak (vague) | ✅ Strong (SMART) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S** | **Specific** | Is the goal clear and concrete? | "Improve my English" | "Pass IELTS with Band 6.5" |
| **M** | **Measurable** | Can I tell if I've achieved it? | "Learn faster" | "Finish 1 course / week, score ≥ 80% on tests" |
| **A** | **Achievable** | Is it realistic for me? | "Become a top engineer in a month" | "Reach intermediate level in 3 months" |
| **R** | **Relevant** | Does it matter to my real goal? | "Learn origami" (irrelevant) | "Learn SQL" (feeds my data-analysis goal) |
| **T** | **Time-bound** | Is there a deadline? | "Someday" | "By Dec 31, 2026" |

### How it connects to the Chapter 1 table
I referenced SMART because **it's the "立标准 / set the goal" step** in the decision flow — and it's exactly what makes the learning methods usable:

- **Simon Learning Method** needs a goal → **SMART** makes it concrete (e.g., "Master AI product design within 3 months").
- **Deliberate Practice** needs a target skill → **SMART** defines what "mastered" means and by when.
- **Forgetting Curve** needs a review schedule → **T** gives it a timeframe.

Without SMART, the methods work but you can't tell if they're *working* — SMART gives you the finish line to measure against.

### A worked example (from the book — 李明 learning AI product design)

| SMART | His goal |
|---|---|
| **S** | Master the core knowledge of AI product design |
| **M** | Finish 3 online courses + pass internal tests |
| **A** | Achievable — 2 hours/day with his current workload |
| **R** | Highly relevant to his current job |
| **T** | Complete within 6 months |

### One-line memory
> **把模糊的愿望，改成"具体、可衡量、可实现、相关、有时限"的一句话** — 这才是能执行、能判定成败的目标。
